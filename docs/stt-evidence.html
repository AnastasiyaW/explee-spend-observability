Read that first number carefully: the reference transcript is this engine's output, confirmed correct on the terms by the operator. Its 152 / 152 is therefore arithmetic, not evidence about it. What the ranking measures is how far each other engine falls short of a transcript a human has confirmed — and none of them reaches half of it.

The whole hour: time × every engine

Each segment is one of the four consecutive 15-minute windows. A row is an exact 25 seconds of audio; the eight adjacent cells are what every engine heard in that same row. Opened on demand, so the page does not draw cells nobody asked for.

The primary metric of this evaluation is not WER. The speech is Russian and the terminology is English. WER barely distinguishes ClickHouse from a Cyrillic transliteration of it, yet for a meeting record that is exactly the difference between a usable and a useless transcript. So the primary score is the share of human-confirmed IT terms recovered in their accepted Latin spelling in the right interval. A Cyrillic transliteration, a plausible wrong Latin spelling and an omission are three different errors, not one.

What this ranking cannot see. It measures agreement with a human-confirmed reference transcript, so a term that every engine including the reference got wrong produces no slot and is never counted. The slots are 25 seconds wide and always contain speech, so a failure means “this engine did not write that term in Latin script here”, not “this engine was silent”. And no Cyrillic transliteration list was confirmed by a human, so a transliterated rendering is counted as a wrong rendering rather than as its own class — the side-by-side view below shows what each engine actually wrote.

Runtime is a tie-break only. Speed proves that a model can finish; it does not prove that it preserved ClickHouse, RAG or a vendor name. A fast model cannot win by turning the important word into a plausible wrong one.

Why speed is only the tie-break. The fastest engine measured here is Meta MMS-1B Russian adapter — 19.125 s for the hour, 139.47 – 214.8997× real time. It also recovers 1 / 152 of the confirmed terms. Ranking by speed would hand it the job; that is precisely the outcome the ordering refuses.

The guess going in was wrong, and the numbers are how we know. Before scoring, the expectation was that the pick would be MOSS or Vosk. MOSS Transcribe Diarize 0.9B agrees on 63 / 152 terms, in 1343.25 s; Vosk Russian 0.42 agrees on 0 / 152 terms, in 709.737 s. Both are near the bottom of the ranking, and the second of them did not produce a single one of the confirmed terms in Latin script anywhere in the hour. A comparison that had agreed with the guess would have been worth nothing.
