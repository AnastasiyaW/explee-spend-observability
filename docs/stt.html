Read that first number carefully: the reference transcript is this engine's output, confirmed correct on the terms by the operator. Its 152 / 152 is arithmetic, not evidence about it. What the ranking measures is how far each other engine falls short of a transcript a human has confirmed — and none of them reaches half of it. A term the reference itself got wrong produces no slot and stays invisible to the method.

Eight variants from six independent STT families processed the same 60 minutes in fixed 25-second, zero-overlap windows. All seven GPU variants passed their admission canary, the CPU-only Vosk baseline completed, and 32 of 32 transcript artifacts were admitted. The operator then confirmed one transcript as correct on the terms, which turned it into the reference for 152 time-bounded term slots; every variant is scored against those.

Why speed is only the tie-break. Meta MMS-1B is the fastest thing measured here — 19.1 s for the hour, 188.2× real time — and it recovers 1 of 152 confirmed terms. Ranking by speed would hand it the job. Speed proves that a model can finish; it does not prove that it preserved ClickHouse, RAG or a vendor name.

The guess going in was wrong, and the numbers are how we know. Before scoring, the expectation was that the pick would be MOSS or Vosk. MOSS agrees on 63 / 152 terms and needs 1,343.3 s where the winner needs 71.5 s; Vosk agrees on 0 / 152. Vosk's zero is not a broken run — it produced 49,602 characters of Russian across the hour and not one Latin-script token in any of it, transliterating every term instead (LSP → «ласпи», JetBrains → «джек брэндс»). That is exactly the failure this evaluation was built to catch, and it is why the metric is term recall in the right script rather than WER.

What “better” means here

  1. Four frozen 15-minute technical segments have the same SHA-256 source authority for every run.
  2. Every transcript must cover the complete duration using the same 25-second, zero-overlap chunk plan; model or device substitutions are rejected.
  3. Systems may propose candidate terms, but cannot grade themselves. A human listens to bounded intervals and creates the reference authority.
  4. The primary score is exact Latin-script recall of the confirmed IT terms. Cyrillic transliteration, a plausible wrong Latin spelling and an omission remain distinct errors.
  5. Runtime is a tie-break only. A fast model cannot win by turning the important word into a plausible but wrong one.
